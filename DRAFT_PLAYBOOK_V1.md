## Quick reference

## Our shared vocabulary

### Player groups

A player can belong to several groups. These describe what we want from a pick, not ESPN position eligibility.

| Group | Meaning | What we check |
|---|---|---|
| **Foundations** | First-pick candidates around whom we can build | Overall contribution, availability, and strategic flexibility |
| **Creators** | Players supplying substantial assists, often with points | Whether assists come with shooting or defensive weaknesses |
| **Scorers** | Players adding points, threes, and/or strong free throws | Whether we still need assists, rebounds, or defense |
| **Bigs** | Players supplying rebounds, blocks, and/or FG% | Actual blocks, FT volume, and the center limit |

“Sleeper” is a separate label: a player whose plausible value exceeds draft cost. It is not a reason to draft someone ahead of clearly better players. We have not established a late-round sleeper list in this version.

### Category status

Use **Strength**, **Competitive**, **Needs help**, or **Punt**. Early labels are tentative. A category is not a Strength simply because one player is good at it; judge the whole roster and the opposition. Punt means we stop paying extra to improve it, not that we refuse all players who help it.

### Team builds

| Build | Main direction | Decision rule |
|---|---|---|
| **Balanced** | Keep all eight categories in play initially | Default until a more focused build offers clear value |
| **Perimeter** | Develop PTS, 3PM, AST, FT%, STL | Consider punting FG% only if repair is costly; continue looking for REB or BLK support |
| **Interior** | Develop FG%, REB, PTS, then AST and defense | Consider punting FT%; deliberately acquire STL and BLK rather than assuming big players supply both |

These are targets, not promised wins. Prefer six competitive categories to relying on exactly five. No automatic second punt. Never say “punt turnovers”: they do not exist in this league.

## Opening scenarios

These are conditional examples, not predictions that a player will be available. Use the ADP reference to recognise when a combination requires someone to fall.

### Early slot: 1–3

**Plan:** Take an available Foundation. Use rounds 2–3 as a complementary pair, not two isolated choices.

- After **Jokić**, compare a Scorer and a source of blocks. Don't spend both picks adding more rebounds while leaving shooting and blocks unattended.
- After **Gilgeous-Alexander**, compare a Creator and a Big. His presence does not mean assists are already secured.
- After **Wembanyama**, prioritise creation. More blocks can wait if useful guards are disappearing.
- Example at slot 2: **Gilgeous-Alexander → Barnes → Towns**, only if available. Check assists at pick 35; Murray is a candidate. This stays Balanced rather than forcing a punt.

### Middle slot: 4–6

**Plan:** Take a falling Foundation first. Otherwise choose between creation, scoring, and an intentional Interior opening.

- **Cunningham opening:** seek a complementary Scorer/wing in round 2 and frontcourt help in round 3. Example at slot 5: **Cunningham → Maxey → Towns** requires Maxey to last beyond his current ADP. If he goes, compare Flagg or Barnes; reconsider the third pick accordingly.
- **Antetokounmpo opening:** mark FT% “Consider punt” immediately. Do not spend the next two picks trying to undo his shooting profile. Example at slot 6: **Antetokounmpo → Barnes → Şengün** requires Barnes to be selected ahead of his ADP and should only beat alternatives on fit/value. This opening still needs blocks and threes; it has not secured five categories.
- If the appropriate partner is gone, take the best remaining tier and reassess. Do not force the example.

### Late slot: 7–9

**Plan:** Build around the combined value of two good players. Do not reach for a center just to fill C.

- Compare **Cunningham, Edwards, Jalen Johnson, Maxey, Mitchell**, plus any earlier Foundation that falls.
- Example at slot 9: **Cunningham → Mitchell** if Cunningham slides. At picks 27–28, compare an available Big and a wing or additional Creator.
- Alternative: **Edwards → Jalen Johnson** if both last. Decide at the next turn whether the larger need is creation or interior defense.
- Two guards do not automatically mean punt FG%. Leave Balanced active until the next pair shows whether that weakness is repairable.

## Punt checkpoint: after picks 3 and 5

Ask these questions before changing the build:

1. Is the weakness substantial across the roster, including shot volume for percentages?
2. Are suitable repair players still available near our next picks?
3. Would repairing it cost better opportunities in several other categories?
4. Which five categories would the new build win, and what is our sixth option?
5. Can we fill the roster legally under the center limit?

Use **Stay Balanced**, **Consider punt [category]**, or **Commit to punt [category]**. A weak first three picks in blocks might need one Big, not abandonment. A high-volume FT% weakness needs a different assessment from a low-volume bad shooter.

At pick 3, prefer “Consider.” At pick 5, commit only if the remaining player pool supports the plan. These are review points, not mandatory deadlines. A punt should make upcoming decisions clearer; don't reverse it after every opposing pick.

## Draft-night routine

Before your turn, queue five available names: best overall value, best complement, alternative Creator, alternative Big/Scorer, and one fallback. Categories can overlap; don't add an inferior player just to fill a label.

When choosing, check: **tier → availability → contribution → roster legality → price**. Within a similar tier, fit breaks the tie. A large value gap can override the preferred group.
